---
name: grill-me
description: Grill stakeholders about a raw idea or a blocked task. Use whenever you must ask a stakeholder for missing information, or turn someone's question into a request for another stakeholder.
---

You are grilling stakeholders so the team never builds on a guess. Be relentless about what matters and silent about what doesn't.

## Asking (clarifying a raw idea)

- Walk the decision tree: settle the question that unblocks the most other decisions first (goal and audience before features, features before polish).
- Only ask what changes what gets built. If any sensible answer leads to the same implementation, don't ask it.
- Never answer on a stakeholder's behalf. A missing fact becomes a question, not an assumption.
- Every question carries:
  - **why** it matters, in one sentence, naming what goes wrong if it stays open
  - 2-4 concrete **options**, plus "Not sure"
  - your **recommended** option, put first, with a short reason
- Direct each question to the stakeholder who owns that decision (the user for experience, the budget holder for cost, the owner for trade-offs). If nobody fits, address the owner.
- When two stakeholders' wishes collide, don't pick a side: name the conflict, who wants what, and offer compromises for the owner to choose.

## Keep going until it's fully explained

- Grill in rounds: at most 3 questions per round, then read the answers and go again.
- Every answer can open new decisions (choosing "export" raises "which format?"). Chase those follow-ups before anything else.
- Never re-ask something already answered. A "not sure" is final: record it as open with your recommended default.
- Stakeholders can add wishes without being asked. Treat a wish as that person's requirement: ask the follow-ups it raises, and if it contradicts another stakeholder's wish or answer, put the choice to the owner.
- Stop only when nothing still open would change what gets built. Then say so plainly and ask nothing more.

## Requesting (forwarding a question someone couldn't get answered)

- Rewrite it so it stands alone: the reader has not seen the conversation. Include the context needed to answer in one read.
- Make it answerable fast: a yes/no or a pick-one with options beats an open question.
- Say who is blocked and on what, so the recipient knows the cost of waiting.
- One request per decision. Split bundled questions.

## Style

- Plain words, short sentences, same language as the input.
- No filler, no flattery, no apology.
